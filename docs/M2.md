# M2 — Store-and-Forward Across a Coverage Gap

**Status: PASS** (13 Sep 2026, WSL2). Bundle sent at T+15s crossed a 50-second total-blackout gap and was delivered at T+102s. Zero loss, zero retransmission.

**Goal:** demonstrate the atom of everything LunaFlow does: a bundle arrives at a relay **while no onward contact exists**, waits in the relay's storage, and is transmitted when the scheduled window opens. This is the mechanism the dawn flush stresses at scale (M5), and the mechanism LunaFlow's policies manage (M6).

---

## 1. Topology & timeline

```
probe (10) ----A----> relay (20) ----B----> ground (30)
  far side              orbit                 Earth

Window A: T+10..40    probe <-> relay   10 kB/s   (surface pass)
GAP:      T+40..90    relay sees NOBODY           <- bundle parked here
Window B: T+90..150   relay <-> ground  20 kB/s   (Earth in view)
```

The lunar physics is *entirely encoded in the contact plan* (`global.ionrc`):

```
a contact +10 +40 10 20 10000     # window A, both directions
a contact +10 +40 20 10 10000
a contact +90 +150 20 30 20000    # window B, both directions
a contact +90 +150 30 20 20000
a range +0 +3600 10 20 1          # one-way light time (s)
...
```

`+N` times are relative to when each node's `ionadmin` loads the plan → start all nodes within a few seconds of each other (the scripts do), or windows skew between nodes.

## 2. Scripts

- `scripts/m2_setup.sh` — generates `~/testbed/m2/` with all three node config dirs (unique keys, ports 4610/4620/4630, neighbor ducts/plans) + shared contact plan. Re-runnable (wipes and regenerates).
- `scripts/m2_run.sh` — the timed experiment: reset → start nodes → bpsink at ground → send at T+15 → inspect relay at T+25 and T+55 → verify delivery after T+90 → PASS/FAIL verdict → teardown. **Warning: uses `killm f`, which kills every ION node on the machine.**

## 4. Why this works (the part to re-read after a break)

- **CGR routes through time, not space.** At T+15 the probe's forwarder computes a route to ipn:30.1 over the *time-expanded* contact graph: contact(10→20, now) then contact(20→30, at T+90). The second hop doesn't exist yet — and that's fine. A route is a sequence of future contacts.
- **The relay's wait is planned, not a failure.** No timers fire, nothing retransmits. The bundle sits in node 20's SDR until `rfxclock` opens contact 20→30 and `udpclo` drains the queue.
- **TTL is the budget for patience.** `-t3600` gives the bundle 1 hour to live. Default is 300 s — enough here, but dawn-flush bundles must survive simulated hours; expired-before-delivery is one of our two loss modes (the other: buffer overflow, M4+).
- **This is exactly what breaks at scale:** one bundle parked at a relay is fine. Six probes dumping backlogs into a relay whose SDR is finite, faster than window B can drain them — that's M5, and the relay's parking lot overflows.
